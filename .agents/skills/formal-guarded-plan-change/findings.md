# Findings of formal-guarded-plan-change

The three findings were replayed against the real code at the sync commit with the Vitest harness of `packages/dms-saas/test` (in-memory MongoDB, the real dms tenant lifecycle, Stripe stubbed at the client). The replay was a scratch test and is not committed.

- **F1, a Stripe schedule request admitted after `effectiveAt` (`DeadlineStopsProviderEffect`, every configuration).** `admitGuardedProviderEffect` (state.ts:112-120) checks the revision and the phase but not the deadline. `admitGuardedCancellation` does (state.ts:140). In TLC's counterexample, the scheduler prepares, pauses, drains, validates, the deadline passes, and the admission lands. In the replay, validation waited until 300 ms after a 3 s deadline. `subscriptions.update` was called once with `{ cancel_at_period_end: true }` after the deadline. The call rejected with `Downgrade provider confirmation requires reconciliation`, because Stripe had renewed. The row stayed in `provider_pending` with the tenant blocked. `cancelGuardedFreeChange` rejected with `Expected downgrade phase scheduled`. `reconcileGuardedFreeChange` answered `true` and left `provider_pending`. With validation ending before the deadline, the same call scheduled cleanly. Filed as AntelopeJS/dms-saas#206. Fixed: `admitGuardedProviderEffect` now refuses past `effectiveAt`, and the replay is committed in `test/guarded-free-downgrade.test.ts` as `aborts and releases instead of scheduling Stripe when validation outlasts effectiveAt`.
- **F2, a stale canceller completes another operation's abort and leaves its barrier held (`AdmissionResumes`, `Races`).** `cancelGuardedFreeChange` re-reads the row before `completeGuardedAbort` and checks only the phase (free.ts:191, state.ts:95-109). The scheduler's catch and the cron compare the operation there. In TLC's counterexample, a canceller aborts operation 1 and stalls. Operation 1 ends, operation 2 prepares and pauses, its drain gives up, its abort lands and its release throws. Then the canceller completes operation 2's abort. The row is clear, so no cron or webhook looks at the tenant again, and the barrier stays. In the replay, the canceller resolved, `guardedPlanChange` was `null`, operation 2's barrier was still held, `reconcileGuardedFreeChange` answered `false`, an inventory write was refused with 409, and a new plan change was refused with `Another inventory preparation is active`. Without the stale canceller, the cron released the barrier and a write was admitted. Filed as AntelopeJS/dms-saas#207.
- **F3, a stalled landing completes another landing's activation and leaves its barrier held (`AdmissionResumes`, full `Faults` only).** After its `finally` releases its own barrier, a landing re-reads the row and completes whatever activation it finds (landing.ts:305-308), its own or not. The CI configuration has one reconciler, so only the full one, with two, reaches it. In TLC's counterexample, landing r1 is refused by a fault and records `blocked`, then stalls after its release. Landing r2 starts from `blocked`, pauses, validates and writes `activating`, and its release throws. r1 then completes r2's activation and clears the row, and r2's barrier stays. In the replay, r1 answered `true`, the plan was Free, `guardedPlanChange` was `null`, r2's barrier was still held, `reconcileGuardedFreeChange` answered `false` and an inventory write was refused with 409. Without r1, the next reconcile released the barrier, completed the activation, and a write was admitted. Filed as AntelopeJS/dms-saas#209. TLC needs about 20 minutes on one worker to find it, which is most of the `--full` run.
